**What made covering fire work historically.** The sources point to five mechanisms. *Exposure vs reaction:* rushes were kept short so the defender could not aim. German 1906 rushes were meant to "leave the enemy no time to fire", and FM 21-75 caps rushes at 3–5 s. Even an alert rifleman on a range needed ~1.3–1.6 s for a first aimed shot and hit a 2-s, 75 m target only 29% of the time. *Fire as the condition of movement:* US regulations launch a rush only while the rest of the line fires "vigorously". SS 143 pairs Lewis traversing fire and rifle-bomb fire on the strong point with a flank attack "without halting". *Area suppression by rate, not hits:* rounds landing within ~1–3 m (5.56) or ~25 m (.50) suppressed half the time, and a 100 m front needed ~90–130 rifle rounds a minute for 50% suppression. Burst interval mattered and burst length did not; random spread beat systematic fire. *Brevity:* suppression lasted seconds to "tens of seconds" after the last close round. In 1915, "as soon as the artillery ceases its fire, the garrison hastily mans the positions", so the assault had to arrive first. *Low lethality under stress:* historical defensive rifle fire reached ~1/10 of trial effectiveness and MG fire ~1/5–1/6. About 2 attack tanks per MG in WW1 cut the defence a further ×10, and for a given weight of shell the shortest, sharpest bombardment cut it most.
