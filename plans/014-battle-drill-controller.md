# Plan 014: battle-drill controller — realistic small-unit tactics without test-fitting

**Status:** user-approved architecture plan, 16 September 2026. Astra implements in phases; Fable reviews each phase on request.
**Author:** Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude-fable-5-1`), direct Claude Code session in this checkout. This is an actual attributed architect response; it was not produced through the launcher. Read-only analysis: no gameplay source was changed and the simulation fingerprint remains `d0e577abab5319b6` (candidate90).
**Companion:** [scenario catalogue, family generator ranges and metric definitions](014-battle-drill-scenarios.md).

## 1. User decisions recorded in this planning session

1. **Implementer.** Astra implements, Fable reviews. This document and its companion are the deliverable; Astra builds in slices and requests bounded reviews per phase.
2. **Hotfix first.** A measured crowding hotfix on candidate90 precedes the new controller. The 28/30 historical score may move; the user accepts that trade.
3. **Acceptance.** Promotion of the new controller uses a distributional scoreboard on rotating validation draws plus the user's replay review. The frozen nine and thirty cases still run and are reported, but they no longer gate or guide tuning. This supersedes the 8/9 and 8/10-per-layout law for the new controller; it stays in force as history for cognition.
4. **Squad organisation.** Two elements inside the eight-man squad. Officers lead the platoon, not a section; a squad is a sergeant and his corporal. One element suppresses (historically the German MG34 group, the British Bren gun group, the American BAR element; Soviet squads rushed with submachine guns as one body) and the other flanks with rifles and submachine guns. Element membership stays fixed for the duration of a manoeuvre and is reorganised only when the current drill completes, otherwise the AI behaves strangely. Loss of key personnel matters: the assistant takes over at once in mission-command armies and only after platoon orders in rigid ones; a downed gunner means the nearest man drops his rifle and gets the gun firing; attrition changes structure (US-style squads collapse to one element under the automatic rifle, German-style squads become a defensive gun nest and stop flanking, British-style squads protect the gun group and halt to merge with another depleted section).

## 2. Problem and evidence

The user chose candidate90 as the main build, played it, and saw squads and soldiers bunching where earlier builds spread out. Measured on Fractured Works, seed 107, 360 s ([normal-map review](../docs/AI_NORMAL_MAP_REVIEW.md)):

| Controller | Median nearest squadmate | Samples within 2 m |
|---|---:|---:|
| candidate90 cognition | 0.95 m | 80.6 % |
| legacy | 7.50 m | 11.3 % |
| earlier 9/9 cognition `3606cbd8375bec12` | 0.98 m | 80.2 % |

Three independent code surveys agree on the causes. Anchors are against `d0e577abab5319b6`; all are in the default path.

1. **Every squad's first objective is the map origin.** `CognitiveSim.cpp:603` reads `command.mission`, which only legacy code writes (`CommandSim.cpp:159-205`, `CoordinationSim.cpp:38`). `PlanSim.cpp:12` returns into cognition before the legacy lane search runs. All eight squads' first accepted routes target `[0,0,0]` at 0.05 s.
2. **One destination, one 10 m bubble, 1.1 m separation.** `Positions()` (`CognitiveSim.cpp:151-202`) gives the whole squad one point, collects peeks within 10 m of it (`:163`), scores each slot by distance to that point (`:183`), rejects only peeks within 1.1 m of a reserved peek (`:174`), and falls back to a two-file block 2 m wide with 1.5 m rows (`:188`). Every eligible member becomes a mover (`:693-697`) although only two occupants are required (`BattleSim.h:185`).
3. **Occupancy rules are dead code.** Typed cognition tasks never reach the 2.0 m shelter and 1.8 m peek ally rejection in `ChooseOrder` (`BattleSim.cpp:552`; the typed path in `TaskSim.cpp:83-86` skips it). The emergency shelter search (`TaskSim.cpp:100-113`) has no ally check, so suppressed soldiers converge on the same cover. Physical separation acts only under 0.9 m (`BattleSim.cpp:879-886`), which is why the median is 0.95 m.
4. **No cross-squad separation.** `UpdateCognitivePlan` never receives other squads' destinations (`PlanSim.cpp:10-12` drops `approaches` and `claimed`); the legacy repulsion terms (`ManeuverSim.cpp:265`, `:152`) and the 48 m search lanes (`BattleSim.h:12`) are unreachable. The platoon sends every non-mover squad to the mover's position plus a fixed offset (`PlatoonSim.cpp:109-115`); in cognition those directives force `Hold` (`CognitiveSim.cpp:665`) and last the whole battle (`PlatoonSim.cpp:56`). The NCO formation refresh is skipped (`CommandSim.cpp:657`).
5. **The tactical vocabulary is five tasks.** Cognition emits only `BoundMove`, `PullBack`, `Overwatch`, `Hold` and `BoundCover` (`CognitiveSim.cpp:721-722`). Fire-and-manoeuvre exists only as a single-gun dependency (`AcceptedPlan::support`, `StageNeedsSupport` at `:82`, `ReceivedThreatSupport` at `:27`). Bounding is a label; the real alternating bounds live in legacy `CoordinationSim.cpp:99-144`. A platoon flank (`PlatoonSim.cpp:62-78`) arrives at the squad as a plain Seize, and there is no suppress-this-area task.

The evaluator that shaped this (`tools/evaluate_routes.py`) passes a case when one accepted route completes and two movers within 8 m of its destination hold a clear physical line to a live defender at 45 degrees or more from one assigned support gun for three continuous seconds. It measures nothing about dispersion, suppression, the route taken or casualties. All 69 acceptance cases are three hand-authored layouts (mirrors and one bend of each other) whose seeds vary only reaction times and dice, never geometry or army. The playable maps have no tactical gate. Seeds 2001-2010 have never run because `tools/run_ai_validation.py` refuses below 9/9 on the tuned set. The thirteen review-and-fix rounds since plan 005 each added a rule to pass specific cases on those layouts. That is the over-fitting the user describes, and it is why 28/30 coexists with 0.95 m spacing.

Already present and reusable: roles (`BattleSim.h:85`), squad succession after a 3 s disruption (`CommandSim.cpp:344-350`) and platoon succession (`PlatoonSim.cpp:133-143`), one support weapon per team in squad 0 slot 7 (`BattleSim.cpp:170`), the fixture-only group position query with spacing, lane and angle constraints (`RecoverySim.cpp:75-165`), the legacy fire-team split and bounding (`CoordinationSim.cpp:7`, `:99-144`), the route planner with corridors (`TacticalRouteSim`), and the method lifecycle with receipts, safety override, resume and exhaustion (`CognitiveSim.cpp:108-123`, `:205-260`).

## 4. Architecture decision

Build a third selectable controller, the **battle-drill controller** (`--drills`, Unreal `-ArmyDrills`), on the retained infrastructure. Legacy and cognition stay selectable and their gameplay digests stay byte-identical. The new controller replaces only the squad and platoon decision layers:

```
Platoon (Lt, PSG)     task tree with priorities and capacities -> manoeuvre choice: fix and flank / support by fire / hold / withdraw
                      areas of operation and corridors per squad; lift and shift triggers; reserve and merge tasks
Squad (Sgt, Cpl)      battle drills as HTN methods: MoveTactically, ReactToContact, SupportByFire, SquadAttack,
                      BreakContact, Occupy, Observe, Withdraw, Reorganise; two elements with frozen membership per drill
Soldier               prioritised goals: Survive > FollowOrder > EngageKnownThreat > KeepFormationSlot > Scan
                      tasks: Rush, Suppress(area, rate), OccupySlot, Overwatch(sector), RecoverWeapon, Shelter, Withdraw
Position service      one query API for all layers: generation, hard conditions, weights, squad-level allocation with
                      spacing and lane constraints, one or two fallbacks per member, position_query traces
```

Retained unchanged: physics and projectiles, perception cone and detection delay, reaction queues, beliefs and mental map, report transport and delays, the tactical route planner and corridors, traffic leases, geometry and maps, diagnostics and digests, and the receipt, interruption and resume execution contract. Knowledge boundary unchanged: every policy input is the officer's own memory, own orders and received reports. Areas of operation and lane claims are the platoon's own intentions and are legal knowledge.

## 5. Squad organisation model

Doctrine templates are data, not per-nation code, because the setting is fictional. A template defines the elements, who leads them, thresholds and succession culture. Templates ship with the controller and are selectable per team; the Build layer can expose them per army later.

```
SquadOrganisation {
  elements[2]: { name, kind: GunGroup | RifleGroup | AssaultGroup, leader: Sergeant | Corporal,
                 preferredRoles, minStrength }
  attrition:   { collapseBelow, collapseTo: SingleElement | GunNest | HaltAndMerge }
  gunNeverSilent: bool            // nearest element member re-roles to a downed support weapon
  succession:  { assistantDelay, riflemanDelay, requiresPlatoonOrder: bool }
}
```

| Template | Elements | Attrition | Succession |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gun-centric | gun group is the core; rifle group feeds and protects it | at 5 or fewer: GunNest, no flanking | fast; any rifleman may lead |
| Section | gun group plus rifle group; gun group protected | rifle group under 3: HaltAndMerge; platoon issues a merge task | assistant fast, rifleman slow |
| Teams | two even teams; either can suppress | at 5 or fewer: SingleElement under the automatic weapon | assistant fast |
| Rush | one assault body; the platoon weapon suppresses | at 4 or fewer: Withdraw or Hold | requiresPlatoonOrder |

Invariants:

- **Frozen elements.** An accepted drill instance carries `ElementAssignment{generation, members, leader}` for both elements. Casualties are removed; nobody migrates between elements while the drill runs. If an element drops below `minStrength`, the drill fails with the existing `InsufficientStrength` cause and `Reorganise` runs before any replan. `Reorganise` also runs in the consolidate phase of every completed drill and reports the new strength upward.
- **Leadership.** The sergeant leads the squad and the corporal assists; the template says which element each leads. Officers appear only at platoon level. Succession delays come from the template and travel through the existing succession path (`CommandSim.cpp:344-350`), replacing the fixed 3 s disruption.
- **Gun never silent.** A downed support weapon becomes a recoverable object at the casualty's position. This is the one new physical mechanic: a dropped support weapon that a squadmate can pick up within his element's area. The gun group's next member receives `RecoverWeapon`; while the gun is silent the base-of-fire requirement is unmet and dependent movement pauses under the existing support-evidence contract. If recovery is unreachable or exposed, the element reports `Blocked` and the drill re-plans with a rifle base of fire.
- **Rifle-only squads.** The current army has one support weapon per team. A squad without one uses a rifle base of fire, and the platoon may attach the platoon weapon to the fixing element. The generator, and later the Build layer, vary support weapons per squad.

## 6. Squad layer: position service and drill library

### 6.1 Position service (`Sim/PositionSim`)

One query API used by all layers: `QueryPositions(leader, members, map, query, time, diagnostics)` returning one primary and up to two fallback slots per member with reasons. Generation: cover catalogue slots plus wall-tangent offsets within a radius of an anchor (a formation slot, the squad centre, an objective or a threat sector). Hard conditions: reachable through the accepted corridor; not within the uncertainty radius of a known enemy track; not inside another member's firing lane (`TacticalPairLaneClear`); spacing from every assigned or seen ally; inside the coherence radius. Weights: cover from known threats, line of fire to the sector, travel distance, exposure of the connector. Allocation is squad-level (Crytek's whole-squad assignment) so no two members receive the same slot, and every query and rejection is traced as `position_query` and `position_pair_rejected`.

### 6.2 Dispersion rules

| Level | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Soldier to soldier | shelter at least 2.0 m and peek at least 1.8 m from any assigned or seen ally; never more than two members within 3 m outside a passage or building | restores the `ChooseOrder` rule for all tasks; one burst, one man |
| Formation | wedge or column projected on the squad route; interval 10 m in the open, contracting to 3-5 m in restrictive terrain or poor visibility, 5 m in an assault line | FM 3-21.8; Verweij |
| Coherence | members constrained to a radius around the squad centre; leading members wait when no valid slot lies inside it | Jack |
| Element to element | base of fire at least 15 m from the assault element and 45-55 degrees at the objective; mutual firing lanes clear | reuse `RecoverySim` `minSupportDistance`, `AssaultAngle`, `TacticalPairLaneClear` |
| Squad to squad | each squad owns an area of operation (lane or corridor); corridors claimed by another squad add route cost; reserve and consolidate positions are queried inside the squad's own area, never the mover's position plus an offset | Killzone path penalty; replaces `PlatoonSim.cpp:109-115` |

The values are doctrinal starting points, not measured optima; they are tunable per template and must be reported with the family metrics, never fitted to a seed.

### 6.3 Drill contracts

Each drill is an HTN method with ordered branches, preconditions on the leader's knowledge, a decomposition into element and soldier tasks with stages, completion evidence, failure reasons and a timeout. The lifecycle keeps cognition's accept, stage, safety override, resume and exhaustion contract and its trace kinds, renamed `drill_*`. Replanning runs at the existing 1 s cadence but keeps the current drill unless a higher-priority branch applies (the Killzone continue branch) or fresh threat evidence changes the committed corridor; switching carries a cost and stable tie order.

| Drill | Trigger and preconditions | Decomposition | Completion, failure, timeout |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MoveTactically(destination, technique)** | platoon task or default sector; route from the planner for the squad, elements offset by formation | technique from expected contact: traveling when no known or reported enemy within 150 m; traveling overwatch when a reported enemy is within 150 m or the route crosses an unobserved corridor mouth (lead element up to 50 m ahead); bounding overwatch when a known enemy is within 100 m or fire has been received (bounds no longer than two-thirds of the overwatch element's weapon range, overwatch halted at firing positions with a line over the bound). Formation: column in restrictive terrain, wedge per element in the open, intervals per 6.2. Members hold formation slots through the position service | complete when both elements report arrival inside the destination radius; contact transitions to ReactToContact (not a failure); fail on Unreachable or InsufficientStrength; timeout from path length over the existing crouched speed plus report allowance |
| **ReactToContact(contact)** | any member receives fire, or the leader learns of a contact within 100 m that threatens the route | all members: nearest protected slot with occupancy, return fire at known contacts in sector; element leaders report; the leader assesses within 2-4 s plus reaction: believed enemy strength (mental map) against own, fire superiority from delivered rounds against received fire. Branches in order: effective incoming fire and no cover -> BreakContact; an element has a line to the threat and strength at or above `minStrength` -> SupportByFire and assault assessment; otherwise hold in cover, report `Blocked/Support` and request platoon support by fire | transitions only; never completes by itself; timeout reports the assessment result upward |
| **SupportByFire(area, element)** | element has at least two members able to reach protected positions with a line to the area | positions from the service; `Suppress(area, rate)` tasks with a sustained rate under the existing reload model; the support weapon, if present, engages the priority target (crew-served weapons first) | established when delivered rounds on the area reach the contract's count inside a 6 s window from at least one element shooter; lost after 8 s without evidence; lift or shift on the assault element's phase-line receipt, with hold-fire lanes across its route; fail on no positions (`Support`), element below `minStrength`, or the gun silent beyond the recovery timeout |
| **SquadAttack(objective, threat)** | assessment says suppression is achievable; a covered and concealed route exists for the manoeuvre element to an assault position at least 45-55 degrees from the base of fire and within 40 m of the objective (planner exposure below threshold from known threats); strength ratio acceptable under template and profile | Prepare: base of fire established. Move: manoeuvre element bounds along the covered route by buddy rushes of at most 5 s between covers; pause if base-of-fire evidence is lost for more than 8 s; entry into any stage within 18 m of the objective or of a known threat is gated on that threat being covered. Assault: line at 5 m intervals; lift or shift signal sent before crossing the base-of-fire lane; assault through the objective by fire and movement. Consolidate: occupy positions oriented on remaining known threats; base of fire moves up. Reorganise | complete when at least half of the manoeuvre element occupies the objective area with receipts and no known live enemy remains inside its radius; one alternate route allowed when a fresh threat exposes the corridor, then `Blocked`; fail on `InsufficientStrength` or support lost beyond timeout |
| **BreakContact(direction)** | believed enemy strength at least twice own with no fire superiority, or three casualties within 20 s without superiority, or a platoon Withdraw order | one element suppresses while the other bounds rearward 25-50 m to the next covered position; alternate | complete when no fresh contact within 100 m for 20 s or the rally point is reached; report strength |
| **Occupy(area, sectors)** | platoon task or consolidation | positions with mutual support and sectors; elements keep separation | ends only by supersession; reports observed contacts |
| **Observe(area)** | platoon task or assessment needs information | cognition's observation semantics retained: a viewpoint with a line inside sight range; a fresh sighting or clearance completes; silence times out with an explicit nothing-learned report | as cognition |
| **Withdraw(rally)** | platoon order or template rule | BreakContact without pursuit, then Occupy at the rally point | as BreakContact |
| **Reorganise** | after every completed or failed drill, on leader or gun loss | re-form elements from survivors per template; re-role to the gun; apply succession; report strength | completes in one plan tick; never runs mid-drill |

### 6.4 Soldier layer

Goals in priority order, each preemptible with receipts: Survive (effective suppression above the existing threshold, shelter within reach chosen with occupancy) > FollowOrder (current assignment) > EngageKnownThreat (fire from cover at known contacts in sector) > KeepFormationSlot (adjust when drift exceeds 1.5 intervals) > Scan. Tasks map onto the existing `Task` and `Completion` pairs initially (transit, occupy with slot, overwatch, rifle support, hold, observe, pull back); `Suppress` reuses the bounded area-fire behaviour proven in reliability fixture 27, and `Rush` is a short bound with at most 5 s of exposure. New enum values are appended only when a mapping does not exist.

## 7. Platoon layer: task tree and manoeuvre

`Sim/PlatoonTaskSim` builds a declarative task tree from the commander's own knowledge. Tasks: Advance(sector) by default, Fix(known enemy group), Flank(objective, side), Secure(sector), Reserve, Merge(squad into squad), Withdraw. Each task carries priority, capacity in squads, an area of operation, an activation condition on the commander's knowledge, a filter (for example needs a support weapon) and status. Squads flow to the highest-priority unfilled task by greedy assignment minimising `H(squad, task)` = travel time along the planner + coordination penalties (a fixing squad without potential lines, a flank without a fix) + tree balance + profile bias - a stickiness credit for the current assignment. Reallocation happens on activation and deactivation events and otherwise at the existing 5 s cadence. Directives expire after 60-90 s with renewal, never the whole battle.

Envelopment procedure on contact: group known contacts (minimum spanning tree, 20 m threshold); flanks are the extreme contacts relative to the platoon centroid; candidate flank objectives lie 30-40 m beyond each flank and require a covered route (planner exposure below threshold); the better side wins by route quality; the fixing squad is the one that can reach positions with lines to the group within its weapon range; by default 60 % of available squads flank inside their own corridors and the remainder holds in reserve in its own area behind the fixing element. Corridor claims add cost to other squads' routes. When no covered route exists: Fix plus Observe plus Reserve, and an honest report upward. Withdraw when believed strength ratio falls below one half with no support. Lift and shift: the fixing element receives the assault element's phase-line receipt through command transport and uses the existing fire-lane messages. Leader death degrades to squad-autonomous drills under the template's succession rule.

## 7a. Platoon redesign — user decision, 17 September 2026

After six Phase 4 builds the user redirected the platoon layer: it was micromanaging squads that already know how to fight. The full amendment is [plan 015](015-squad-initiative-amendment.md). In short: the platoon issues three intents only (fight here, retreat there, help that squad); squads choose flank, fire and movement, suppression or break contact themselves; squads coordinate over the platoon radio with the existing report delay; a second squad that finds a route joins an assault from its own side; and the pass bar is the user's replay review with automated guards, the candidate90 casualty and closure targets being withdrawn. Section 7 above is superseded except for order persistence, the believed-ratio withdrawal, expiry and renewal, and succession.

## 8. Metrics

Computed offline from `evaluation.jsonl`, `events.jsonl`, `trace.jsonl` and the new `shots.jsonl`; observer facts never feed policy. Exact formulas are in the companion document.

- Spatial: nearest-squadmate distance median and share under 2 m; squad radius; minimum inter-squad centroid distance; area-of-operation compliance.
- Tactical conformance: share of moving-soldier seconds covered by friendly delivered fire on the relevant known threat area in the preceding 6 s; assault arrival angle relative to the base of fire; exposed seconds without a firing solution; suppression established before assault; break contact when outnumbered; friendly-fire hits; gun-silent seconds after a gunner casualty.
- Outcome: win, draw, loss; casualty exchange ratio as fractions of initial actives; time to decision.
- Command: contact-to-order latency, order reversals per minute, directive churn, succession latency.

## 9. Anti-over-fitting protocol and promotion

1. **Scenario families instead of layouts.** `Sim/ScenarioGen` produces maps, deployments and defender compositions from `(family, genSeed)`: F1 defended position, F2 meeting engagement, F3 urban block, F4 trench line. Generation uses its own RNG so `--seed` stays comparable across scenarios.
2. **Rotating validation draws.** Development draws are fixed and open. Validation genSeeds derive from `hash(candidate fingerprint, family, salt)`, run once per candidate, are never reused, and every case is reported.
3. **Distributions, not counts.** Per family: outcome, conformance and spatial metrics against fixed sparring partners (legacy, candidate90, the previous drills candidate) with bootstrap confidence intervals.
4. **Mechanism scenarios with paired controls per drill.** Listed in the companion document.
5. **Frozen evaluator retained as regression only.** The nine and thirty cases run and are reported; they are neither a tuning target nor a promotion threshold.
6. **Change discipline.** A behaviour change is accepted only as a doctrine rule motivated by a mechanism scenario and checked on the development family distribution, never on one seed. Per-case fixes are refused in review.
7. **Human review.** The user watches replays on both playable maps before any main-build change; the user's judgement of realism is the final gate.

Promotion of the drills controller requires: no regression against candidate90 on outcome within confidence intervals on every family; conformance and spatial metrics better than both baselines; every mechanism scenario and control passing on Linux and native Windows; legacy and cognition digests unchanged; trace on and off parity; replay review by the user; `AI_MAIN_BUILD.md` updated with candidate90 preserved as selectable.

## 10a. Phase 0b outcome (16 September 2026)

The candidate90 crowding hotfix was built, measured and reviewed in three iterations (`plans/014-phase0-fable-review.md`, `014-phase0b-fable-review.md`, `014-phase0b2-fable-review.md`). It met the spacing target (Works nearest-squadmate median 8.27 m against 0.98 m) but made soldiers passive: Trenches fell to 228 shots per battle against candidate90's 799 and legacy's 1989, and Works casualties halved. The user's replay review confirmed one or two men doing the shooting and no movement to cover. The hotfix is rejected as a playable build and archived at `.local/phase0b/hotfix-final.patch` with its evidence. The user chose legacy as the interim playable default while the drills controller is built; candidate90 remains selectable and preserved.

Lessons carried into every later phase:

- **Engagement intensity is a hard exit criterion.** No slice passes with shots per battle or casualty fractions below candidate90 on either playable map, reported per run. "No zero-shot battle" is not a criterion.
- **Constraining cognition trades aggression for spacing.** Occupancy blocks become waiting, covering rules become holding, and its method library has no drill that closes with the enemy. Spacing and aggression must come from drills designed together (Phases 2 and 3), not from patches on the old controller.
- **Review the replay before trusting a metric table.** The user's ten-minute look found what the exit rule missed.

## 12. Review protocol

Each phase request states objective, source fingerprint, changed files, the mechanism scenarios with their paired controls and bounds, the family report against both baselines, and the parity evidence. Fable checks, in this order: no hidden-state reads in policy; no per-case rule (a change must name its drill, its scenario and its family effect); legacy and cognition digests unchanged; trace on and off parity; element membership frozen mid-drill in traces; the frozen 9 and 30 reported but not used to justify a change. Review is advisory, not acceptance; promotion criteria are in section 9.

## 13. Out of scope and risks

Out of scope: campaign systems, hearing, anonymous-contact association, capacity-limited communications, new weapons beyond the recoverable support weapon, persistent progression, learned controllers. Risks: the drill library is authored doctrine and will look wrong in situations the doctrine never covered, which the family metrics and replay review are designed to surface; a data-driven template system can still be over-fitted if thresholds are tuned per seed, which the change discipline forbids; Windows and Linux floating-point results differ, so parity is per platform as today.
