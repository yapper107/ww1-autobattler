## Highest-impact problems

**1. The box exchange visibly switches objects outside the pouch.** At **62→63 (31.567→31.600 s)**, the empty box disappears while its broad face is still exposed in front of the bag wall/rim. The hand stays near the opening; no intervening descent conceals the box. At **69→70 (31.800→31.833 s)**, the loaded box and projecting rounds appear together at that exposed location. The native `pouch-native.png` sequence makes both changes unambiguous. This is a demonstrated continuity/prop failure, not an inference from hidden contact. Route the whole box through the actual mouth, hide any ownership change inside the bag, then show the replacement rising through the opening. The visible path and concealment must work before timing the switch.

**2. The glove does not clearly adopt the different working poses.** Native **92–104 (32.567–32.967 s)** shows a similar open, scooped silhouette over the feed, with curled fingertips projecting upward. A related silhouette persists after closure through **114–127 (33.300–33.733 s)**. The hand travels with the operation, but does not visibly distinguish belt placement, a closing press and a firm charging grip. The cover's upward/downward motion is readable at **28–37** and **106–117**; its exact contact edge is partly hidden by the glove and cuff. I therefore do not claim a proven floating gap or penetration there. The concrete authoring target is a purposeful wrist orientation and grasp change for each operation: grasp/guide the belt, release it into the feed, apply pressure to the cover, then acquire and release the charging handle. Preserve the current connected elbow route.

**3. Weight and support remain weak.** Through roughly **40–104 (30.833–32.967 s)**, the operating arm removes the box, drops to the thigh and returns while the weapon and torso remain comparatively fixed. There is little distinct bracing or counteraction when support leaves the gun, when the box seats, or when the hand works above the receiver. This is a visible performance limitation, not proof that the hidden far hand has lost contact. Give the supported weapon and shoulder a restrained, mechanically motivated response rather than treating every operation as an isolated arm gesture. The large open thigh bag also remains conspicuously rigid through the exit; its attachment and movement deserve a later check.
