## Remaining quality problems and concrete next targets

**1. Make the braking support unmistakable before deep compression.** In both close views around **33–41, 19.100–19.367s**, the leading boot appears separated from its ground-shadow area while the trailing boot is also raised. The body is already low. [Male frame 35](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-080224/combat-0035.png) and [female frame 35](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-080313/combat-0035.png) expose this most clearly. The long, low phase reads somewhat suspended or gliding before the stronger support impression around **43–50**. It is continuous; it is not another single-frame pop.

This is a rendered support concern, not a claim that a particular foot is a measured distance above the floor. Confirm the actual contact from a low side view with clear ground landmarks. If the sole is airborne, bring the plant ahead of the strong body loading; if it is already contacting, strengthen the visible heel/sole compression and pelvis progression over it. Preserve the smooth swing path rather than forcing a new instantaneous foot lock. A short flight phase is normal during sprinting; the concern is the low, prolonged braking configuration and weakly legible load-bearing contact.

**2. Carry the braking load through the upper body and weapon.** During **30–50, 19.000–19.667s**, the knees and pelvis make the largest change. The rifle changes angle and stays attached, but the shoulder/trunk/weapon assembly has relatively little distinct catch and delayed recovery. The result still resembles an armed upper pose carried by a more active lower-body stop. Add restrained shoulder/trunk absorption timed to the established plant, then controlled recovery of the weapon's mass. Arbitrary larger muzzle motion or broken grips would not help. The four-body course shows the same limited weight differentiation with the bulkier MG.

**3. Reduce the coat's prolonged rigid flare.** Most visibly on the male in **12–41, 18.400–19.367s**, the hem is held far behind in a broad, nearly horizontal shape, then collapses toward rest during **43–55**. Cloth should trail the sprint, but this long, similarly shaped flare makes the body/garment response feel less integrated. A more progressive, damped change through deceleration would help. This is secondary to reliable support and body load response; the stylized garment itself is not being penalized.
