1. **Entry is continuous, including the exact former boundary.** Current local **14→15** (43.967→44.000 seconds) preserves the carry and body posture on both rifles and both MGs. The weapon then changes angle through **16–20**, while the head and trunk begin responding. Old **1319→1320** immediately presents a different arm/weapon pose, particularly obvious on the rifles. The new multi-frame change is a real improvement. I found no replacement one-frame whole-body or gun reset in the inspected entry. The fast changes at 17–20 are connected poses, not evidence of another atomic pop.

2. **The body still pauses between reaction and collapse.** After the initial recoil/bow, all four stay in a very similar bowed, hands-cupped posture for approximately **27–47** (44.400–45.067 seconds). The knees then descend mainly through **49–59** (45.133–45.467), with a kneeling beat around **60–64**, a separate forward fold through **65–75**, and the side roll through **76–85**. This reads as successive posed stages rather than a convincing continuous loss of support. The raised empty palms are especially conspicuous on the male bodies; the female silhouette shows the same rhythm. A slower wounded collapse can be intentional, but this version needs readable muscular failure, balance transfer and impact response to motivate its holds. The brief feet-up reaction around **24–29** is not automatically wrong; I am not treating every airborne pose as a defect or estimating sole heights from soft shadows.

3. **Release and ground motion remain weak, with a demonstrated MG collision failure.** The hands open while the gun lies across the forearm/cuff region around **21–26**; separation becomes clear through **27–29**. The release does not read as a clean grip loss, and the barrel/cuff overlap deserves an opposite-side contact check rather than assuming every overlap proves penetration. Once free, all weapons descend almost level through **29–35** (44.467–44.667). After floor arrival, they roll/rock through substantially different long-axis orientations in **36–43**, reverse orientation through **44–52**, then rotate again through **54–60**. This is more accurately described as repeated rolling/rocking than simply a pitch bounce. The stock repeatedly rises without a persuasive diminishing impact response. The heavier MG has essentially the same light-looking behavior. More decisively, its attached green ammunition box is cut by the floor at **35** and again through **49–54** (45.133–45.300), leaving only a thin part of the belt/box visible below the receiver. This is visible ground penetration, not ordinary occlusion by a hand or body.

4. **The MG belt pouch also penetrates the ground.** In both MG captures, the large yellow pouch reaches the floor during **75–76**. Through **76–81** (46.033–46.200), its lower volume is progressively sliced away by the ground as the torso rolls. Thin yellow surfaces remain around the hip at rest. The visible plane cutoff establishes an equipment/ground failure; it is not merely hidden on the far side of the body. This is an additional reason for the MG scores being lower. The rifle variants lack that large pouch, though their final body/weapon overlap still cannot certify the hidden collision surfaces.

5. **The final transition is continuous and the tail is quiet.** The side roll and small settling motion around **76–93** do not introduce an obvious single-frame pose switch. Frames **93–164** (46.600–48.967) remain settled without a visible late reset or renewed large motion. Quiet does not mean contact-correct: the MG pouch penetration persists. The final roll has limited visible cushioning and equipment response, and the supporting hand is mostly palm-up rather than making a convincing protective brace. This is a performance concern, not a requirement that a dying person must catch the fall.

The highest-impact next corrections are: **(1)** connect the bowed reaction to the knee and torso failure with a deliberate weight trajectory and better motivated timing; **(2)** author a clean hand release and weight-appropriate weapon impact/settling path that respects the complete rifle/MG geometry; **(3)** make the MG pouch and other equipment clear or deform against the ground throughout the side roll. Preserve the repaired entry while doing those changes. Further polish of the first blend alone will not address these blockers.
