## What works and what remains unproven

The rifle stays associated with the supporting left hand across the captured sequence; I found no obvious large support-hand detachment in the reviewed views. The shoulder/torso inclines and head lowers during the reload, so there is some bodily participation rather than a completely isolated arm. The action has a discernible order: work the action, reach down, open/access pouch, bring ammunition up, work over the receiver, discard the strip, and recover. The final firing silhouette is established by roughly 164–166 and remains stable through 179. The early shot/bolt repetitions also have visible rifle/head/arm changes; I cannot certify the recoil's real-time weight from contact sheets.

**The right pouch flap does visibly open.** Front 111 begins its lift; 113–121 shows it raised; it is descending around 123–125 and closed again by approximately 127. Ammunition is visible in the hand by 119 and clearly visible in 121–125. A claim that the flap never opens, or that no ammunition is shown, would be incorrect.

The supplied interrupted-reload interval begins while the right hand is already near the action. The adjacent images show a coherent route into the pouch reach, without a gross whole-pose teleport. That does not establish that the interruption blend feels natural in continuous playback. Similarly, the final return's sampled path is coherent, but the repeated hand gesture and weak grip settlement remain visible.

## Acceptance judgment

On the requested scale, this is a **functional intermediate animation at 5.5/10**, with identifiable mechanical beats and substantial contact/articulation work still needed. The strongest blockers are the bolt-hand silhouette and obscured feed; the weakly communicated bolt-driven ejection is another staging issue. These problems are central to the action, not isolated hidden-frame nitpicks. A finger repair should be judged in new captures; it receives no assumed credit here, and it will not automatically resolve staging, clearance, or load response.

I would not reasonably call these exact captures AAA studio-ready. An 8.5 requires the hand–weapon interactions to remain convincing throughout the action and the full sequence to hold up in uninterrupted gameplay-speed playback. This report neither reaches that threshold nor certifies the playback portion of it.
