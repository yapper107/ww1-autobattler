# Pass 50 — machine-gun performance comparison

26 September 2026. Independent rendered-evidence review by the same critic who completed pass 49. No animation or implementation source was edited or used to determine the grades.

**Provisional grades: female 6/10; male 6/10.** Both improve on the previously reviewed 5.5/10 performance, chiefly because the weapon remains in a credible firing position when movement begins. Neither reaches the requested 8.5 threshold. The longer views expose a substantial late gait interruption, and the body still communicates too little of the weapon's weight and firing force.

The user's anchors remain unchanged: the rejected original is 3/10; 8.5 means fluid, well-executed AAA/TLOU2-level motion. These grades assess the animation within the stylized art, without penalizing the characters for lacking photorealistic detail. They are qualitative judgments, not instrument measurements.

## Highest-impact remaining work

1. **Make the walking and stopping performance belong to one consistent gait.** Full-body frames approximately 310–350 (36.33–37.67s) depart sharply from the preceding walk: the rear leg lifts much higher, the body takes running-style steps, then sinks into a deep braking crouch with the rear knee close to the floor. That low shape persists for multiple frames before rising into recovery. The weapon remains aimed, so the upper and lower body communicate different levels of effort. See [female 317, 36.567s](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-072920/combat-0317.png), [female 340, 37.333s](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-072920/combat-0340.png), [male 317](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-073042/combat-0317.png), and [male 340](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-073042/combat-0340.png). Use a walk-compatible deceleration and footfall sequence, retaining the improved firing brace. Judge the repair through the complete approach, stop and return to standing. The images establish the visible mismatch; this report does not verify an animation-asset or code cause.

2. **Make firing force travel through the supporting body.** Native crops of full frames 0–11 and 24–35 reveal small, quick changes in gun angle. Recoil is therefore not simply absent. However, the head, shoulder mass, trunk, pelvis and planted legs change little while the gun oscillates, and there is little visible distinction between resisting a burst and recovering from it. Examples: [female frame 1, 26.033s](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-072920/combat-0001.png), [female frame 3](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-072920/combat-0003.png), [male frame 1](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-073042/combat-0001.png), [male frame 3](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-073042/combat-0003.png). Strengthen the connection between stock/arms, shoulder compression and a restrained trunk response, with a distinct controlled recovery. More muzzle excursion alone would not solve this. Keep the firing line usable during the walking steps.

3. **Give the reload's load changes and contact operations clearer consequences.** The added dip helps, but much of 30–34s still reads as an arm performing successive tasks over nearly the same lower-body base. Establish a small preparatory weight shift as the support hand leaves, then coordinate the supporting shoulder, trunk and pelvis as the gun and box change leverage. Within that action, distinguish box seating and cover closure through approach, visible commitment and release. Frame 98 feed work through frame 124 closure is the useful close-view window; [female high frame 124, 33.633s](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-072721/combat-0124.png) and [male high frame 124](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-072820/combat-0124.png) anchor its end. Preserve the current continuous box path and stable support. Additional ornamental gestures would add activity without establishing weight.

The full-body views support a positive statement about stationary reload footing: the boots maintain their ground relationship without an obvious repositioning pop. They do not justify certifying every walking foot plant or asserting a measured sliding distance. The most certain lower-body finding is the large late pose/gait change.

## Comparison scope and record

Pass 49 covered 29.5–35.467s and largely excluded the feet. Pass 50 adds 26–29.5s and 35.5–38.967s, with the entire body visible. The late braking defect is newly exposed evidence, not an established regression against pass 49. In the shared window, the maintained firing brace and modest reload dip are real gains. Across the fuller performance, the remaining gait interruption and weak force transmission keep both bodies well below 8.5.
