## Turning: a pause followed by late footwork

Both bodies preserve a small initial adjustment around **16–22 / 7.033–7.233s**. The rifle remains coherently carried. There is no demonstrated gross one-frame shoulder, weapon or whole-body snap in either 90-frame sequence.

This is not a completely frozen pose, but the small adjustment does not progress into a readable, coordinated turn. The head, trunk and carried gun remain in nearly the same heading for a conspicuous interval.

From approximately **48 / 8.100s**, heading advances again. The boots are initially still close and flat-looking. On the male, the base opens during **55–61 / 8.333–8.533s**; the most obvious unloading and crossing step comes later at **66–72 / 8.700–8.900s**, after much of the heading change has occurred. On the female, the opening appears around **60–65 / 8.500–8.667s**, followed by a smaller heel/foot adjustment around **66–70**. Her feet and body still largely rotate together without a strong accepting-leg/pelvis transfer.

The late male step is real articulation and should be preserved where appropriate. Its timing currently reads as catching up with orientation rather than driving it. The female has less readable separation of those roles. A short pivot can be physically plausible without a large step, but this evidence does not make its load-bearing point and release sufficiently clear.

In the held-turn comparisons 082622/082713, heading continues advancing through **30–45**. Those poses retain their own close-foot, weak-support limitation, but the current near-hold and resumed rotation introduce a new interruption of the action's flow. Pass 54 likewise shows foot opening earlier in its main rotation, while remaining insufficiently grounded. None of these versions is accepted; simply replacing the prior uniform swivel with a pause does not resolve it.

The body, pelvis and rifle still have little distinct sequencing around support. The carry grips remain coherent in the exposed view, and **75–89** proceeds into renewed locomotion without a gross jump. Hidden palm surfaces are not certified.

**Next correction:** make one visible support exchange explain the heading change. The current sequences already start in a low crossed-foot approach. During **0–11 / 23.500–23.867s**, the boots pass close together beneath the leaned torso. During **12–25 / 23.900–24.333s**, a boot swings outward and the base opens progressively. This is visibly earlier than 081746, where the body stays more tightly tucked/crossed until roughly **24.433s** and opens afterward. The current event order is closer to 080401's earlier opening through approximately **24.000–24.333s**. That specific pass 55 regression is improved.

The wider phase continues through **25–31 / 24.333–24.533s**, then the feet/body settle toward a compact crouch during **32–39**. **40–45** holds that crouch, and **46–51 / 25.033–25.200s** rises progressively. Neither body demonstrates a return of the old one-frame deep-kneel substitution or a new abrupt leg split within this window. The short rise is fast, but adjacent poses form a continuous path; speed alone is not a pop.

There are still substantial quality limits. The initial crossed configuration is narrow for the low, sideways-moving body. In native **12–24**, especially on the female, the swinging boot stays angled above the floor for a long part of the step while the other ankle has a toe-led support silhouette. A raised swing foot is normal; the concern is the weakly legible pelvis transfer over the accepting foot and the prolonged low posture, not an assertion that both feet must be down. Exact support and sole slip need a complementary low view before a hard geometric failure claim.

The shoulders and rifle remain comparatively fixed while the legs perform the stop. The resulting compression has limited visible load absorption, and the brief settled crouch offers little recovery of the carried mass. The male coat hides more of the upper legs; the female exposes the same basic timing more clearly. These are performance limitations, not reasons to penalize the stylized character shapes.

**Next correction:** retain the earlier opening, then make the accepting sole and pelvis transfer clearer and reduce the cramped crossed approach. Add restrained torso/shoulder absorption timed to that support, preserving the rifle contacts. Review the complete crouch action and a close ground-level companion before treating this short restoration as an accepted family.
