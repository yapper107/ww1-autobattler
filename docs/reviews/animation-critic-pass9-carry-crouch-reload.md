## Carry and walking

The rifle sits closer to the body with a clearly downward muzzle. Both gloves remain visually associated with the rear grip and fore-end in the inspected poses, including **f0, f60, f84, f102, f142, f150, f162 and f195**. I do not see a gross disconnected hand gap in those views. Exact finger-surface relationships and the occluded stock/chest interface cannot be certified from this camera.

This is a meaningful improvement over the earlier extended, uniformly forward presentation. The carry silhouette is more credible for sustained movement, and the start across **f56–79 (1.867–2.633 seconds)** progresses continuously rather than switching the whole pose in one frame.

The upper body still changes comparatively little as the legs stride and the body brakes. The ready span **0–2 seconds** reads predominantly as a held reference pose. During walking, particularly **f84–138 (2.800–4.600)**, rifle and forearms retain very similar relationships despite substantial stride changes. The useful correction is modest shoulder, elbow and chest response to the load and gait, with the hands remaining connected. Large arm swing or arbitrary sway would undermine the improvement.

The walking stop is the weakest part of this first capture. At **f142–153 (4.733–5.100)** the body brakes with a pronounced backward lean while the feet gather tightly and appear toe-biased. The rifle/arm shape remains close to the carry reference while the lower body absorbs the change. The subsequent **f154–164 (5.133–5.467)** step spreads into the held stance, but does not retroactively make the initial braking support convincing. Adjacent frames show progression; this is a support and performance problem, not a claim of a single-frame teleport.

**Correction target:** stage a clear final plant ahead of the pelvis, absorb the deceleration through that supporting leg, place the recovery foot and settle. Add restrained arm/weapon follow-through that reflects the stop. Preserve the closer, lowered carry. Review it against ground references before asserting that planted feet are stable.

## Crouch and stop

The new lowered carry reduces the conspicuous upward muzzle flourish identified in pass 8. It does not resolve the underlying weight transfer. At **f102–110 (24.400–24.667)**, tightly gathered/crossed feet sit beneath a strongly pitched torso. Through **f110–118 (24.667–24.933)**, the base spreads and the torso straightens toward idle, followed almost immediately by the standing rise at **f121–125 (25.033–25.167)**. There are continuous intermediate poses through the inspected stop and rise.

The moving character also remains heavily hunched, with the head directed down over the weapon through much of **f48–102 (22.600–24.400)**. The upper body communicates little change of attention or response to the different steps. The new weapon pose is more compact, but the whole body has not become a convincing continuous crouched walk/stop performance.

**Correction target:** author pelvis travel and supporting-knee compression around a clearly established plant, then settle the recovery foot into an idle compatible with that support. Keep the head and weapon intent coherent. Show a held stop as well as stop-to-rise; the present short hold makes it difficult to separate stopping quality from the following rise. The focused grade stays 5.0.

## Reload

There is a real spatial improvement over pass 7: at **f300–309 (31.000–31.300)** the glove now visibly meets the front of the pouch, rather than hovering forward under the trigger area. That resolves the broad placement objection, but contact with the pouch face is not yet a convincing retrieval.

At **f310–317 (31.333–31.567)** the elbow lifts outward while the glove remains low across the front of the pouch. The subsequent **f318–323 (31.600–31.767)** brings the hand toward the receiver. I cannot clearly follow a grasp at the pouch opening, extraction of ammunition, and transport into alignment. It still resembles a held contact pose followed by a reach. The correction must involve the glove/fingers and prop path, not merely a palm position that touches the pouch.

**Correction target:** reach to the actual top opening, establish the grasp, extract upward far enough to clear the pouch, and carry the visible ammunition to the receiver with continuous orientation. Avoid the outward elbow lift becoming the dominant gesture while the hand remains fixed on the pouch face. Verify on the male body separately before assuming the result transfers.

Insertion at **f324–345 (31.800–32.500)** remains visibly associated with the receiver and avoids the old large gap. It still has a long similar-pose quality; finger/wrist pressure and supporting-arm response remain rudimentary. Recovery through **f366–386 (33.200–33.867)** raises the operating hand back toward the chest/weapon and then aim, but the closing operation still lacks a clear purpose in the silhouette. The sequence needs readable mechanism work and economical reacquisition, followed by a longer outgoing transition review. The reload grade remains 4.5 despite the improved pouch alignment.

## Acceptance boundary

The immediate priorities are believable stop support, actual pouch extraction, and coordinated upper-body load response. These captures do not establish the male low-ready/crouch/reload, either machine-gun body, or the full four-body course. They do not establish prone, crawl, grenade or dive performance. Earlier reports and scores are preserved. No runtime, implementation or source assets were edited by this review.
