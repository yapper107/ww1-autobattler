# Pass 49 — fresh female / male machine-gun motion comparison

26 September 2026. Independent rendered-image review; no animation or source edits.

**Provisional grades: female 5.5/10; male 5.5/10. Neither reaches the requested 8.5.**
The reload is understandable and the visible prop sequence largely holds together. The
largest remaining gap is performance: the body does not convincingly carry the changing
load during the reload, and the exit reads more like lowering into a moving carry than
committing to machine-gun fire. The two bodies have substantially the same strengths and
limitations. Their different proportions and degrees of occlusion do not justify different
overall grades on this evidence.

The user defines the rejected original as 3/10 and 8.5 as fluid, well-executed AAA / TLOU2-level
motion. Those are the anchors used here. This is a subjective grade of this short rendered
sequence, not a measured quality score, a grade of the whole animation library, or AAA
acceptance. The low-poly art style itself is not a deduction.

## Method and limits

All four runs contain `combat-0000.png` through `combat-0179.png`, each 1600 × 900, and each
has `capture-complete.txt` containing “Animation diagnostic capture. Visual approval is
separate.” I inspected **all 720 frames in chronological order**, using 36 sheets of 20
consecutive frames, followed by 48 selected, unresampled native-size crops. The sheets
reduce a foreground crop for sequence inspection; they are not substitutes for the native
contact checks. Four original full-frame images were also inspected directly.

The supplied time mapping is `29.5 + frame / 30` seconds: reload 30–34 seconds; movement /
burst begins at 34. Thus the capture contains roughly 1.47 seconds after the moving-fire
boundary. This is not sufficient coverage for a verdict on an extended firing or locomotion
loop.

**My tools exposed still images, not continuous-video perception. I did not watch actual
normal-speed playback.** Consecutive images support judgments about pose progression,
visible spacing, contact, and action staging; they do not establish how fluid the result
feels at playback speed. There is no audio evidence here. Most boots / floor contacts are
outside the frame, so planted-foot quality and sliding remain unverified. Obscured finger,
stock, and palm surfaces remain unverified unless an exposed view supplies evidence; an
occluded contact is not counted as a demonstrated defect.

Freshness caveat: I did not open prior critic reports or animation implementation files.
While reading the required worktree `AGENTS.md`, I incidentally encountered its stale
Current state line “critic 4.0/10.” Therefore this was not a perfectly blinded review.
The judgments and grades above were written from these images, without consulting other
reviewers' reasoning or grades.

## Three highest-impact changes

1. **Author the firing commitment and its load path.** Keep the end of reload, target
   reacquisition, and first steps as one intentional action. Establish a chosen, readable
   firing brace and preserve it as locomotion starts. Give the shot / burst an observable
   compression and controlled recovery through the arms, shoulder / torso, and stance,
   with the weapon recovering toward its firing line. The present rise-then-lower shape
   is the most consequential mismatch between the named action and visible performance.
   More muzzle effects would not resolve this animation problem.
2. **Make the reload a whole-body response to changing support.** Before the working hand
   releases, let the supporting side take the load. Use a modest coordinated change in
   pelvis position, ribcage, supporting elbow, and gun attitude through the pouch reach,
   retrieval, and seating. Return that load as the offhand takes support again. Preserve
   the readable prop sequence; do not simply add generic sway everywhere. This can be
   the same action logic on both bodies, adjusted for their proportions.
3. **Give the existing manipulation beats distinct force and timing.** Differentiate
   grasp / lift, weighted box transit, seat / press, and cover closure through meaningful
   speed changes, brief contact commitments, hand shapes, and small driven responses of
   the gun and torso. Reduce the impression of repeated similarly shaped forearm arcs.
   The priority is the cause-and-effect of the current actions, not more ornamental
   finger or head movement. Recheck the exposed grip surfaces from both sides after
   changing these beats; do not solve an unproven occlusion problem by distorting a pose.

Next review should retain synchronized main and opposite close views and add an uncut,
normal-speed full-body view through several steps and bursts. The full-body view is for
judging weight transfer, foot contact, and the firing transition; the close views are for
confirming the manipulation. A human normal-speed review is still needed for the stated
fluidity / AAA acceptance target. These images do not justify a pass at 8.5.
