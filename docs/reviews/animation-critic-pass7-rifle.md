## Shot/bolt — both 4.0/10

The compact recoil and return remain usable ingredients. The left hand continues to read as supporting the fore-end, and the weapon has intermediate poses through the cycle. The principal failure from passes 3 and 5 remains plainly visible, however.

At **26.400–27.000 seconds (f12–30)** the operating glove stays beside or beneath the face, over the rear of the weapon. On the female body, the arm and glove crowd the cheek/shoulder silhouette. On the male body, the upward-facing fist is especially prominent and reads more readily as a gesture near the face than a grip manipulating the bolt knob. Later cycles at approximately **27.600–28.267 (f48–68)** and **28.933–29.467 (f88–104)** repeat this problem. Consecutive frames show small continuous movements, but do not make unlock, rearward travel, forward travel and lock convincingly distinct.

The lowering and elbow-clearance changes have therefore not resolved the central visual defect. I cannot establish reliable visible glove-to-knob contact throughout these phases from the rendered view, and proximity to the mechanism is not enough to call it successful. The long near-face portion also gives both performances a held-pose quality. Enlarging recoil would not solve that.

**Next authoring correction:** build the hand, wrist and forearm poses around the visible knob on each actual body. Establish a credible grasp, rotate to unlock, translate with the bolt, push forward and rotate to lock, then return to the firing grip. Test a wrist orientation that leaves the fist below/outside the knob and allows head clearance; this is a proposed experiment, not an accepted repair. Judge the complete glove surface and finger silhouette, not a wrist target. Keep the compact recoil and fore-end support while repairing the operation. Render the mechanism side and the current three-quarter view so the result must work in both.

## Reload — both 4.5/10

The gross action is legible: lower the rifle, look toward the work, reach down, return to the receiver, insert, then reacquire aim. At **31.800–32.500 seconds (f174–195)** the loading prop visibly bridges glove and receiver and descends toward the gun on both bodies. The large hand-to-receiver gap from pass 3 is absent. The male prop is particularly readable in this view. This supports the higher female reload grade relative to pass 3, but not a promotion beyond the quality already represented by the male pass 5 grade.

The remaining problems are concrete:

1. **Pickup is still not established.** At **31.000–31.600 (f150–168)** the female glove rests under the trigger area and in front of the belt. The male glove is lower, below/in front of the pouch. Neither sequence clearly shows a grasp at the pouch opening followed by extraction. At f156 (31.200), both are useful diagnostic poses: hand placement differs between bodies, and neither reads as convincing retrieval. Author the complete reach-contact-grasp-extract-transport sequence against each body's actual pouch geometry.
2. **Insertion is connected but posed.** Across roughly **31.900–32.300 (f177–189)** the upper body remains very similar while the loading prop does much of the explanatory work. The finger and wrist action, pressure into the receiver, and supporting arm response need clearer phrasing. Avoid adding arbitrary sway merely to create movement.
3. **Recovery still contains an unexplained hand gesture.** At **33.200–33.800 (f216–234)** the operating hand rises toward the upper chest/face before the final firing grip, especially on the male body. The necessary closing operation does not read clearly enough to explain this path. Tie the recovery to visible mechanism work and direct reacquisition. The short ending at 34.067 seconds cannot establish the whole transition into moving fire.

These two focused rifle captures do not establish machine-gun performance, the full four-body course, or the actions absent from that course. Normal-speed and slow playback, both useful mechanism views, and complete incoming/outgoing transitions remain necessary before a studio-ready claim.
