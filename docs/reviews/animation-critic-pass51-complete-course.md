# Pass 51 — complete four-body course

26 September 2026. Independent visual assessment of the actual male/female rifle and machine-gun bodies. No animation source or assets were edited.

**Whole-course grade: 5.5/10.** This is a material improvement over my pass 1 course grade of **4.0/10**, but remains well below **8.5/10**. The user's anchors are unchanged: the rejected original is 3; 8.5 means fluid, well-executed, continuous AAA studio-ready motion, with The Last of Us Part II as the stated standard. The stylized models are not penalized for lacking photorealism. I did not view external TLOU footage for this review.

The largest gains are the closer armed carry, higher and more coherent crouch, recognizable rifle operation, and articulated MG reload. The principal remaining failures are a demonstrated sprint-stop discontinuity, weak foot-supported turning and body response, and a staged death performance. A successful close-up weapon operation does not make the complete movement system an 8.5.

## Three highest-impact corrections

### 1. Repair the sprint-stop support transition, then the planted turn

In the final capture, **[frame 583, 19.433s](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-074544/combat-0583.png) → [frame 584, 19.467s](/mnt/c/Users/Jordan/Documents/Codex/ArmyPrototype-GASP/Saved/Screenshots/GaspCombat-20260926-074544/combat-0584.png)** changes both male bodies from a narrow running stance with one leg raised into a broad bracing split. The female feet also rapidly close/cross as the body changes into the stop. The immediately preceding 582→583 change is small; the following frames settle out of the newly imposed shape. This is a demonstrated one-frame discontinuity, not merely a fast but continuous braking action.

It exists in both 073822 and 074544. The improved ordinary walking stop does not resolve it. Preserve the outgoing support and swing-foot path while entering the brake; establish the next planted foot before loading the broad stop pose. Review the whole 19.1–20.2s approach and settlement, not only a corrected pair of frames. The rendered evidence establishes the defect; it does not establish which source, warping or contact layer caused it.

The turn also needs an authored support change. In **frames 230–250, 7.667–8.333s**, the trunk and legs rotate together while both boots look largely grounded; **250–254** then draw the stance inward without a clearly readable lifted step. The grid makes this more apparent. This is a continuous but physically weak pivot, not another claimed single-frame pop. Give the turn an explicit unload, pivot or short repositioning step, with the pelvis following the supported foot. Do not simply add upper-body motion over the same foot path.

### 2. Make the body carry and absorb the weapon's load

The current held gun positions are much better, and carrying a rifle correctly should constrain arm swing. The remaining issue is the small differentiation in shoulder, trunk and pelvis behavior across travel, firing and contact work.

During **13–18s** the legs and coat show considerable locomotion while the arm/weapon assembly remains comparatively uniform. During **26–30s** and **34–38s**, gun impulses and rifle operating motion are visible, but there is little corresponding shoulder compression or controlled trunk recovery. This is especially noticeable with the MG's bulk. Preserve the usable firing line while adding restrained, timed absorption through the supporting body; larger arbitrary muzzle motion would not solve it.

The MG close views show a coherent sequence: the box passes above the pouch rim before lowering, remains associated with the hand through the visible portions of transport, and returns under the receiver. Native frames **52–55 / 60 / 65 / 69 / 72** support that improvement on both bodies. Feed/closure frames **95–116** retain coherent elbows and sleeves. I did not demonstrate the old gross wrist collapse or an exposed box teleport here. The palm and latch interfaces are partly hidden and remain unverified in detail.

The next authoring target is the consequence of those contacts: a small preparatory support shift when the hand leaves the gun, coordinated compensation as the box changes leverage, and a distinct seat/press/close commitment and release. There is already head/trunk dip; the criticism is its limited weight and differentiation, not its absence. The course's 34s transition also starts the next burst, so an impulse there cannot automatically be called an exit snap.

### 3. Reauthor death as a connected loss of support

The final course confirms the original weakness. At **1319→1320, 43.967–44.0s**, the rifle changes abruptly from low carry into a more horizontal presented position as death starts. Through **1333–1340, 44.433–44.667s**, it descends in a nearly level attitude after the hands separate. The body then remains in almost the same hunched, partly supported posture through roughly **1342–1353, 44.733–45.1s**, before kneeling and folding forward.

These are visible staging and timing problems, not a requirement that every dropped weapon tumble identically. Carry the existing hand/weapon motion into release; let the drop and body imbalance belong to one event. Coordinate knees, pelvis, trunk and hands so support fails and transfers continuously, with a legible final ground impact. The current synchronized result looks deliberately lowered in several phases. Inspect all four bodies and both gun masses when revising it.

The later stop opens a support stance and rises without the old return to a deep kneel. This is a genuine rendered improvement, not credit for implementation effort.

**The latest normal and moving-fire stops are more coherent.** In 074544, adjacent frames **138–184** and **1074–1153** show ordinary footfall completion and settlement rather than the conspicuous deep braking action previously exposed in MG review. There are still small foot adjustments, but I did not demonstrate an equivalent one-frame split in these inspected ranges. Do not revert this improvement while correcting the separate sprint failure.

**Weapon actions now have identifiable operations.** The rifle's operating hand, ammunition retrieval/feed sequence and recovery, and the MG's articulated mechanisms make the largest difference from the original course. The reviewed armor remains attached without the earlier gross arm-following plate rotations. Fine receiver/finger contact hidden by the wide camera is a coverage limit, not grounds to repeat previously repaired contact failures.

**Vault/landing is continuous in the inspected evidence.** Free-hand support on the obstacle and a controlled weapon-side clearance read across the sequence. Adjacent 073822 frames **1224–1280** contain a continuous tuck, release, descent and landing; the final course survey shows the same broad performance. Around **1264–1272, 42.133–42.4s**, the weapon is already returning to carry while the legs absorb the landing. More connected trunk/weapon load response would help, but there is no basis here for inventing a new landing teleport. The block hides approach leg details; those are unverified.

## Remaining coverage before an 8.5 decision

The visible failures already prevent acceptance. Separately, this inspection leaves normal-speed rhythm/impact perception, all current rifle close contacts at the default 2.5s duration, varied interruption phases, different approach speeds/directions, terrain/cover contact and landing directly into locomotion insufficiently covered. The course rifle reload occupies the 30–34s review window; historical actual-duration captures 065220/070230 do not automatically certify the current whole system.

Prone, crawl, grenade and dive are absent from this course and are **unreviewed, not passing**. A scripted flat-grid course does not establish integrated battlefield animation quality. These are remaining evidence requirements, not extra numerical deductions for motion that was not shown.
