## Findings on the same criteria

**Support and footwork improve on both bodies.** The opening adjustment around frames 31–39 (7.533–7.800 seconds) has clearer knee flexion and a more substantial boot path than pass 56. The later trailing-foot lift and placement at frames 45–59 (8.000–8.467) are easier to read as an actual support exchange. By frames 60–74 (8.500–8.967), the feet occupy a broader stance than pass 56's narrow, raised-trailing-heel finish. This gives the turn a more resolved endpoint. The female silhouette exposes the knee changes particularly clearly; the same action is visible under the male coat. That visibility difference does not justify different scores.

**Entry continuity is the main new reservation.** After the quiet preceding stance, frames 31–34 (7.533–7.633) bring the chest and rifle briefly toward the camera. Frames 35–38 (7.667–7.767) then reverse that direction into the main turn. The feet and knees also change quickly during this short beat. Counterrotation can be useful anticipation, but here the quick excursion reads more like a correction because there is little visible preparatory loading before it. This is a multi-frame path concern, not a claimed one-frame teleport or a diagnosed code cause. Pass 56's entry was less expressive but more directionally consistent.

Consecutive evidence: [male rifle entry, frames 27–32](/home/jchan/.codex/worktrees/gasp-soldier-animation/ww1-autobattler/.local/animation-review/pass58-sized-turn/male-carry-a-native.jpg), [male frames 33–38](/home/jchan/.codex/worktrees/gasp-soldier-animation/ww1-autobattler/.local/animation-review/pass58-sized-turn/male-carry-b-native.jpg), [female frames 27–32](/home/jchan/.codex/worktrees/gasp-soldier-animation/ww1-autobattler/.local/animation-review/pass58-sized-turn/female-carry-a-native.jpg), [female frames 33–38](/home/jchan/.codex/worktrees/gasp-soldier-animation/ww1-autobattler/.local/animation-review/pass58-sized-turn/female-carry-b-native.jpg).

**Weight transfer remains underdeveloped.** Improved leg articulation does not yet make the main torso rotation feel fully driven by the supporting leg. During frames 39–53 (7.800–8.267), heading progression is comparatively even while the feet pass through a narrow exchange. At the broader final placement, the visible pelvis/chest response is still small. The result is more credible than pass 56 but retains a mechanical quality above the legs. I am not asserting measured foot sliding, penetration or a precise centre-of-mass violation from these images.

**Rifle carriage remains coherent.** The exposed support hand travels with the fore-end and the trigger-hand region remains associated with the receiver. No clear hand separation or independent weapon jump appears in the reviewed crops. The entry direction change affects the carried rifle and torso together. Contacts obscured by the gun or body cannot be certified. The held rifle silhouette is consistent through the main turn, though its restrained response also contributes to the subdued upper-body weight impression.

## Highest-impact next changes

1. **Resolve the entry direction change.** Connect the current stance to the chosen turning action with a coherent chest/rifle path. If retaining anticipation, establish supporting-leg load before the counterrotation and make its timing purposeful. Review consecutive frames 27–41 and actual playback; do not hide the issue by merely slowing the whole turn.
2. **Connect support exchange to body weight.** Make pelvis loading, knee compression and chest response correspond to the leg accepting weight, especially through frames 39–59. Preserve the improved broader placement while giving the final arrival a controlled, visible settling response.

The equal 6.5 scores reflect a real but limited gain in footwork and finish, offset by the entry reservation and remaining weak weight expression. Verification of this revision in the complete course and genuine continuous playback remains necessary before broader claims.
