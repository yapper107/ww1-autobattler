## Shot and bolt cycles

The compact recoil remains readable. In the first cycle, hand approach at **f6–10 (26.200–26.333)** leads into a raised operating position at **f11–13 (26.367–26.433)**. Rearward travel through **f14–18 (26.467–26.600)** is followed by forward travel through **f19–23 (26.633–26.767)**, then downward rotation through **f24–28 (26.800–26.933)**. The glove returns to the firing grip through **f31–34 (27.033–27.133)**.

These stages are easier to distinguish than the earlier extended, similar-pose operating interval. The glove stays visually associated with the mechanism rather than forming the old detached upright fist beside the face. Later cycles in the chronological samples retain that general association. This supports a modest focused increase from 5.0 to **5.5**, not a large quality jump.

The operating hand still resembles a relatively fixed hook shape over the mechanism. Exact finger enclosure, pressure and release are not convincingly readable, and the lock lacks a strong physical completion cue. Much of the body and supporting arm remain held while the operating arm performs its path. The next improvement should make the grip, wrist rotation and modest supporting-arm response express the work being done. Adding arbitrary body sway would not address this.

## Transition into reload

There is no whole-pose jump at **f119→120 (29.967→30.000)**. Hand movement and lowering of the rifle progress through **f124–140 (30.133–30.667)** without an obvious forced low-ready reset.

However, **f108–123 (29.600–30.100)** already presents the character back in the aimed pose. Consequently this capture does **not** demonstrate interruption during an active pull, push or lock. A continuous transition from recovered aim is useful evidence, but cannot validate the more demanding interruption claim. Show reload requests at distinct visible phases of the bolt action before considering that coverage complete.

## Pickup, insertion and recovery

At **f150–159 (31.000–31.300)** the glove is visibly near the pouch's upper region. This is better placed than the old broad contact against the pouch face. The opening and ammunition grasp remain obscured by the hand, arm and stock from this view; I cannot clearly follow a grasp, an item clearing the opening and its uninterrupted transport to the receiver.

The largest immediate defect is the arm excursion at **f160–167 (31.333–31.567)**. The operating elbow rises sharply across the chest over **f160→162**, while the glove remains low near the pouch/stock, then the elbow lowers again through **f163–167**. Intermediate poses exist, so this is a very fast two-frame upward excursion followed by reversal, not a claim of a one-frame teleport. It makes the elbow movement dominate the retrieval. Pass 9's outward elbow problem remains visible despite the improved pickup target.

**Authoring correction:** establish the grasp at the actual opening, move the glove and ammunition upward far enough to clear it, and then transport them toward the receiver. Keep the elbow on one restrained, continuous arc that follows that hand path. Remove the abrupt outward/upward excursion and reversal. A second pouch-facing view is needed to establish the prop's departure and grip; the present occlusion must not be counted as success or as proof that the prop is absent.

Insertion through **f174–195 (31.800–32.500)** remains associated with the receiver and avoids the former large floating gap. It still spends a long interval in similar poses. The press, seating and release need clearer wrist/finger action and a small, physically motivated response from the supporting arm. Receiver association is a necessary improvement, not a complete loading performance.

Recovery still has excess motion. After the lower operating position around **f210–219 (33.000–33.300)**, the hand rises toward the upper chest/neck through **f221–235 (33.367–33.833)** before returning to the firing grip around **f236–239 (33.867–33.967)**. The closing operation is not sufficiently legible to justify that gesture. Author an economical, visibly purposeful close/lock/regrip sequence, then bring the rifle into aim with the grip established. This remaining performance problem and the pickup excursion keep reload at **4.5**, unchanged from pass 9.

## Next priorities and acceptance boundary

1. Correct the pickup's rapid elbow excursion and make grasp, clearance and ammunition transport visible.
2. Replace the chest-height recovery gesture with clearly readable mechanism completion and direct firing-grip recovery.
3. Improve bolt grip/lock expression and demonstrate phase-varied shot-to-reload interruption.

This female rifle capture does not establish the same changes on the male body, either machine-gun body, the full 46-second course or the missing prone/crawl/grenade/dive actions. Continuous playback and broader coverage remain required for the user's 8.5 standard. Earlier reports and scores are preserved. This review changes no runtime code or source assets.
